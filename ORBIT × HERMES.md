# ORBIT × HERMES
# Investor Intelligence Operating System

## MASTER PRODUCT & UX VISION

---

# 0. CRITICAL DIRECTION

We are not building a conventional real estate website.

We are not building a Golden Visa brochure.

We are not building a property marketplace.

We are not building a chatbot with a website attached to it.

We are building an **AI-driven investor acquisition, intelligence, qualification and advisory operating system**.

The customer-facing website is only one interface of that system.

The system's central intelligence and orchestration layer is:

# HERMES

ORBIT is the customer-facing brand and experience.

Hermes is the intelligence and operating layer behind ORBIT.

The long-term system should be capable of understanding:

- where an investor came from
- why they came
- what they are looking for
- what they already know
- what they care about
- what they can potentially invest
- how ready they are
- how they make decisions
- what information they need next
- which opportunities may be relevant
- which advisor or partner should handle them
- when and how the next human interaction should happen

The core product philosophy is:

> **Understand the investor before deciding how to engage with them.**

# 1. BUSINESS CONTEXT

ORBIT focuses initially on the Greek real estate and Golden Visa market.

The primary commercial objective is to connect qualified international investors, especially investors from Turkey, with suitable real estate opportunities and the relevant specialists required to complete the investment and Golden Visa journey.

The initial objective is not to automate the entire investment process.

The initial objective is:

> **Build a high-quality, intelligent investor pipeline and connect the strongest opportunities to humans at the right moment.**


# 7. HERMES SHOULD ANSWER FIVE QUESTIONS

For every meaningful investor interaction, Hermes should progressively answer:

### 1. WHO?

Who is this person?

### 2. WHY?

Why are they here?

### 3. WHAT?

What are they looking for?

### 4. HOW READY?

How close are they to taking action?

### 5. WHAT NEXT?

What should happen now?

The system is successful when the fifth question can be answered intelligently.

# 9. SEARCH INTENT IS A SIGNAL, NOT A FACT

This distinction is critical.

If a person searches for:

- luxury yacht
- Rolex
- luxury property
- private jet
- premium real estate
- high-end travel

we may identify potential purchasing-power or lifestyle signals.

But we must NEVER automatically conclude:

> This person is wealthy.

Likewise:

> luxury search = Golden Visa intent

is not a valid conclusion.

Instead:

```text
External Signal
      ↓
Hypothesis
      ↓
On-site Behavior
      ↓
Assessment
      ↓
Validated Investor Signal
```

Hermes should distinguish:

- observed information
- declared information
- externally sourced information
- inferred information

Never represent inference as fact.

# 16. MINIMAL QUESTIONS, MAXIMUM INTELLIGENCE

The user should never be interrogated.

Core principle:

> **Ask only what Hermes cannot reasonably know from other signals.**

The system can learn from:

- acquisition source
- search intent
- landing page
- navigation
- content interaction
- property interaction
- return visits
- assessment answers
- stated budget
- investment objective
- timing
- Golden Visa familiarity
- CTA behavior

Therefore a user might answer 4–6 questions while Hermes builds a much richer profile.

# 17. ADAPTIVE QUESTIONING

Questions should change according to previous answers.

Example:

### First question

> **What brings you to Greece?**

Options:

- Golden Visa
- Property Investment
- Both
- Exploring

If:

**Golden Visa**

then ask:

> **How familiar are you with Greece's Golden Visa?**

If:

**Property Investment**

then ask:

> **What matters most in your investment?**

If:

**Both**

If:

**Exploring**

# 37. DATA ARCHITECTURE

### DECLARED

Information explicitly provided by the investor.

### OBSERVED

Information derived from first-party behavior.

### EXTERNAL

Information obtained from permitted external sources.

A model-generated hypothesis.

Example:

```text

DECLARED

OBSERVED

EXTERNAL

INFERRED
```

# 41. THE MOST IMPORTANT UX PRINCIPLE

The user should never feel like they are being interrogated.

The system should feel like:

> **A highly intelligent advisor who already understands context and only asks what is necessary.**

Bad experience:

> What is your budget?  
> What is your nationality?  
> What is your income?  
> What is your family size?  
> What is your timeline?  
> What is your investment goal?  
> What is your preferred property?  
> What is your Golden Visa knowledge?

Better experience:

> **What brings you to Greece?**

The next question should depend on the answer.

The next question after that should depend on the new information.

Hermes should continuously decide:

> **Do I already know enough?**

If yes:

> Stop asking.

Move toward the human.
